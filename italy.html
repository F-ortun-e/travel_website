ROMAN COLOSSEUM, ITALY

Standing in the heart of Rome, the Roman Colosseum is a monumental symbol of the ancient world's architectural prowess.
Built between 70-80 AD under the emperors Vespasian and Titus, the Colosseum was the largest amphitheater ever constructed and could hold up to 80,000 spectators.
The structure is a marvel of engineering, featuring a complex system of vaults and arches that allowed for rapid entry and exit of crowds.
The Colosseum hosted gladiatorial contests, public spectacles, and other events for nearly 400 years before falling into decline.
Today, it remains one of the most recognizable landmarks in the world and a testament to the grandeur of ancient Rome.
